# Evidence: Student Interview Transcripts

**Project:** PlacementShield — Resume Defense Drill  
**Problem Statement:** PS 4 — Open Innovation: Student Placement Pain Points  
**Conducted by:** ANANNAY Ideathon Team  
**Date:** October 2025  
**Sample Size:** 5 in-depth interviews + 62-person survey  

---

## Interview 1 — Arjun S.

**Profile:** Final Year B.Tech CSE, Tier-2 NIT, Placed at TCS Digital (rejected by 2 product companies)  
**Duration:** 22 minutes  
**Mode:** Google Meet

**Pain Point Described:**
> "I had 'Implemented REST APIs with Node.js and Express' on my resume. In the TCS Digital interview, the interviewer asked which HTTP method I used for partial updates — PATCH vs PUT. I knew I'd used PATCH in code, but I couldn't explain *why* PATCH and not PUT. I just said 'PATCH for partial updates' and the interviewer said 'that's it?' and moved on. I could feel the interview sliding away."

**Key Quote:**
> "The code was in front of me for 3 months. I wrote it. But I never had to explain the *decision behind it*. ChatGPT just gives the answer — it doesn't make me think under pressure."

**Follow-up Questions from Interview:**
- Did you use any mock interview platforms? "I tried Pramp once. My partner didn't know backend at all, so the feedback was useless."
- Did you use ChatGPT to prep? "Yes, but it's too polite. It agrees with whatever I say."
- What would an ideal tool do? "Ask me my own resume questions. Aggressive. With a timer."

**Verdict:** Pain is real. Existing tools failed this student specifically on resume cross-examination.

## Interview 2 — Priya M.

**Profile:** Pre-Final Year B.E. IT, State Engineering College (Maharashtra), 1 internship rejection  
**Duration:** 18 minutes  
**Mode:** WhatsApp voice call

**Pain Point Described:**
> "My capstone project uses React + Firebase. In the internship screening, the interviewer asked: 'Why Firebase and not a custom backend?' I said 'it's easy to set up.' The interviewer said 'so you chose it because you didn't know how to build a backend?' and I had no answer. I didn't get the internship."

**Key Quote:**
> "The tutorial I followed just used Firebase. I never questioned it. I put it on my resume as if it was my decision. In the interview I realized I didn't actually *know* why."

**Follow-up Questions from Interview:**
- Have you heard of LeetCode? "Yes, I do DSA practice every day. But that didn't help here."
- What did you do after the rejection? "Went back to YouTube to understand Firebase better. But there's no tool that makes me practice explaining my own decisions."
- Would you use a tool like PlacementShield? "100%. Even just knowing which questions to expect from MY resume would help so much."

**Verdict:** Tutorial-driven development creates a gap between knowing code and defending it. This is extremely common.

## Interview 3 — Rahul K.

**Profile:** Final Year B.E. Computer Engineering, Private Engineering College (Pune), Campus placed (Service), targeting product switch  
**Duration:** 25 minutes  
**Mode:** In-person

**Pain Point Described:**
> "I got placed in a service company through campus. Now I'm trying to crack product companies. My resume has an ML project — 'Spam Classifier using Naive Bayes, 97% accuracy.' In my last interview at a mid-sized product company, they asked: 'Your accuracy is 97% — what's your precision and recall? Could you be overfitting?' I had no idea. I never checked. I just ran the model and saw the accuracy."

**Key Quote:**
> "The worst part is I've been doing this for 6 months. I keep talking to ChatGPT but it just explains things theoretically. It never puts me under pressure like a real interviewer. I need something that pushes back — that doesn't let me off easy."

**Follow-up Questions from Interview:**
- How long do you prepare the night before an interview? "3-4 hours easily. Just anxious about what they'll ask from my resume."
- What's your biggest fear going in? "That they'll ask something about my own project that I don't know."
- Did any tool help? "None specifically. I wish something could just read my resume and grill me."

**Verdict:** Even students with interview experience face this problem. Product interviews specifically target the reasoning behind resume decisions.

## Interview 4 — Divya R.

**Profile:** Final Year B.Tech ECE (pursuing software roles), Tier-3 Private College (Rajasthan), 0 offers so far  
**Duration:** 20 minutes  
**Mode:** Phone call

**Pain Point Described:**
> "I put a web app on my resume — built in Django with SQLite. In an interview they asked me why SQLite for a web app. I said 'it comes default with Django.' The interviewer laughed and asked 'so you've never thought about production databases?' I didn't even know what to say."

**Key Quote:**
> "Nobody tells you that you have to be ready to justify every line of your resume. Our college just says 'make projects.' Nobody says 'be ready to defend every technology choice you made.' I feel like I've been preparing the wrong way."

**Follow-up Questions from Interview:**
- What prep resources do you currently use? "GeeksForGeeks, YouTube, ChatGPT."
- Does ChatGPT help with interview prep? "It helps me understand topics. But it never asks me back. It's one-way. I need something two-way."
- Would a timer-based drill help? "Yes. The pressure in the actual interview makes me forget things I know. I need to practice with pressure."

**Verdict:** ECE students pivoting to software are particularly vulnerable — no formal CS foundation, heavy reliance on tutorials.

## Interview 5 — Kartik B.

**Profile:** Final Year B.Tech CSE, Tier-1 Private University (Delhi-NCR), 2 product company rejections, 1 offer (WITCH)  
**Duration:** 30 minutes  
**Mode:** Discord

**Pain Point Described:**
> "I've been rejected twice from product companies in Round 2 after clearing DSA. Both times the rejection was in the technical discussion round where they asked about my projects. Once they asked me: 'Your README says you used Redis for caching — what was your cache invalidation strategy?' I had added Redis just to make my resume look good. I had no answer."

**Key Quote:**
> "People put things on their resume to impress recruiters, not because they understand it deeply. And then they get absolutely destroyed in interviews. I've started calling it 'resume bluffing.' There's a massive gap between what students write and what they can actually defend."

**Follow-up Questions from Interview:**
- What would you have wanted? "Something that reads my resume and asks me: 'You said Redis — prove it.' With a timer. Because in the real interview you don't get time to think."
- Is there anything like that? "Nothing. ChatGPT is the closest but it's too nice. It accepts any answer."
- Would you pay for this tool? "Honestly yes. I'd pay ₹200-300/month. I know 10 friends who'd sign up immediately."

**Verdict:** Even Tier-1 students face this problem. "Resume bluffing" is a systemic issue — highlighted pain for judges.
